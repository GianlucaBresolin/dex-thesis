This thesis presents the internship experience carried out at Inkwell Data Ltd
over a period of six months, focusing on the design and development of DEX, an
Elixir-based framework for data exploration across heterogeneous data sources.

The project involved the development of a configurable and distributed framework
capable of integrating different databases and Elixir applications through a
unified abstraction layer. DEX enables efficient query execution in distributed
environments by distributing the workload across multiple nodes and providing
aggregation mechanisms to combine partial results into a single final response.
This approach abstracts the physical location and underlying technologies of
data, addressing data distribution and governance challenges in increasingly
heterogeneous and decentralized environments.

In addition to abstracting data location, DEX provides a higher-level interface
for expressing data exploration requests without requiring direct knowledge of
SQL or NoSQL query syntaxes. Data exploration is represented through
object-filter-based diagrams, which allow users to navigate relationships
between data entities and progressively define filtering and exploration
criteria. Rather than exposing the underlying database schemas to end users, DEX
allows organizations to define a higher-level representation of their data
through object definitions built on top of the underlying schemas. End users
interact exclusively with these organization-defined objects and their
relationships, without gaining visibility into the actual database structure or
storage mechanisms. This separation enables organizations to provide data
exploration capabilities while retaining control over how their data is
structured and exposed.
